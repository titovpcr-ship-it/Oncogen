# Armenian cases made simple
> Eastern Armenian has seven cases, but the endings are short and regular. We go through them with the word տուն (house).
Armenian has more cases than English but they are far more regular than in Russian or German: most words take the same endings and there is no grammatical gender. Let's use **տուն**, "house".
{{noun: տուն}}
## Nominative and accusative
The basic form: **տուն** — house. For things, the accusative looks the same: "I see the house" — **տունը տեսնում եմ**.
## Definiteness: -ը / -ն
Armenian distinguishes "a house" from "the house": **տուն** — a house, **տունը** — the house. After a vowel the ending is **-ն**.
## Genitive and dative: "of the house", "to the house"
One form for both. Most words take **-ի**: **հաց → հացի**. **տուն** is irregular: **տան**.
## Ablative: "from"
Ending **-ից**: **տնից** — from the house, **Երևանից** — from Yerevan.
## Instrumental: "with, by"
Ending **-ով**: **տաքսիով** — by taxi.
## Locative: "in"
Ending **-ում**: **տնում** — in the house, **Երևանում** — in Yerevan. It is not used for people.
## Plural
One-syllable words take **-եր**, longer words **-ներ**: **տներ** — houses, **քաղաքներ** — cities. Case endings come after it: **տներից** — from the houses.
## "My" and "your" inside the word
**տունս** — my house, **տունդ** — your house. Very common in speech.
## How to remember
Learn ready-made words instead of tables: **Երևանում** (in Yerevan), **տնից** (from home), **տաքսիով** (by taxi). Every noun in Barik with a Wiktionary table shows all its forms — for example [տուն](/en/w/tun.html).
